      预测是怎么算的

      漂亮的晚霞需要四件事同时成立。每个地点、每个模型都分别计算这四项，再相乘得到 0–100 的观赏评分。

      1. 云层“画布”：观景点上空，以及日落方向 25 km、75 km 处的中高云覆盖率。约 45–70% 最理想；完全晴空仍有金色落日（约 20 分），中高云满天则阳光照不进来。
      2. 光路通畅：沿太阳方位角在 75、150、250 km 远海采样低云。夕阳要从地平线下方照亮云底，远海若有云堤，太阳会提前“没入”云中。这是只看单点天气的预报最常漏掉的一项。
      3. 本地遮挡：观景点及近岸的低云、海雾、能见度和降水。加州全球模式常低估海洋层，所以额外用“强逆温 + 高湿度”识别海雾，并给 3 km 分辨率的 HRRR 更高权重。若观景点海拔高于估算的海洋层顶（来自 HRRR 各气压层云量），则视为“雾上”，可能看到云海。
      4. 空气透明度：CAMS 气溶胶光学厚度（AOD）。轻度气溶胶影响不大，烟霾重时颜色发闷。

      评分 ≠ 概率

      观赏评分表示“按各模型的预报，晚霞大概会有多好”（五个模型评分的加权平均）。发生概率表示出现明显晚霞（评分 ≥ 55）的可能性：先看有多少模型给出这种结果，再按预报时效与该地区、该月份的气候基准做收缩。两者可以差很多，例如评分中等但模型一致时概率反而稳定；评分很高但只有一个模型支持时，概率会偏低。

      概率是根据模型间分歧做的启发式估计，尚未用实际观测校准，仅适合横向比较。

      置信度

      综合模型分歧、可用模型数量、预报时效（明天比今天低）、HRRR 是否可用、数据缺失，以及“海雾临界”情形（雾线进退最难预报）。

      地区差异

      北海岸（North Coast）、湾区（Bay Area）、中央海岸（Central Coast）、南加州（SoCal）、圣地亚哥（San Diego）分别使用各自逐月的海雾频率和晚霞基准概率；每个地点都用自己的坐标、海拔和开阔海平线方向计算，而不是用一个城市代表整条海岸。

      已知局限

      • 模型云量为网格平均（3–25 km），无法刻画云的纹理或破碎程度。
      • 海平线方向和海拔为人工标注的近似值，未计算精细地形遮挡。
      • 雾线位置、雾层厚度是最大的不确定来源；出发前请再看一眼实时卫星云图或网络摄像头。
      • 数据在云端每小时更新一次；各气象模型通常每 1–6 小时发布新一轮预报。

      How the forecast works

      A great afterglow needs four things at once. For every spot and every model, each is scored separately and multiplied into a 0–100 viewing score.

      1. Cloud canvas: mid/high cloud cover above the spot and 25 km and 75 km out toward the sunset. Around 45–70% is ideal; a cloudless sky still gives a golden sunset (about 20 points), while an overcast deck keeps the light out.
      2. Light path: low cloud sampled 75, 150 and 250 km out along the sun’s azimuth. The setting sun lights clouds from below the horizon; a cloud bank far offshore makes it “sink” early. This is what single-point forecasts most often miss.
      3. Local view: low cloud, fog, visibility and rain at the spot and just offshore. Global models routinely underestimate California’s marine layer, so a “strong inversion + high humidity” signal is used to detect fog, and the 3 km HRRR model gets extra weight. If the spot is higher than the estimated marine-layer top (from HRRR cloud cover at pressure levels), it is treated as “above the fog”, with a chance of a sea of clouds.
      4. Air clarity: CAMS aerosol optical depth (AOD). Light aerosols barely matter; heavy haze or smoke mutes the colors.

      Score ≠ chance

      The viewing score is how good the sunset should be if the models are right (a weighted average of five models). The chance is the probability of a notable glow (score ≥ 55): it counts how many models produce one, then shrinks toward a baseline for that region and month depending on lead time. The two can differ a lot: a middling score with agreeing models gives a steady chance, while a high score backed by a single model gives a low one.

      The chance is a heuristic built from model disagreement and is not yet calibrated against observations — use it to compare spots, not as a true frequency.

      Confidence

      Combines model disagreement, the number of models available, lead time (tomorrow is less certain than today), whether HRRR is available, missing data, and “marginal fog” situations (the hardest call on this coast).

      Regional differences

      The North Coast, Bay Area, Central Coast, SoCal and San Diego each have their own monthly fog frequency and baseline chance of a good sunset. Every spot uses its own coordinates, elevation and open-horizon direction — no single city stands in for the whole coast.

      Known limitations

      • Model cloud cover is a grid average (3–25 km), so cloud texture and breaks are invisible to it.
      • Horizon directions and elevations are hand-annotated approximations; detailed terrain blocking is not computed.
      • Where the fog line sits and how thick the fog is are the biggest uncertainties — check a live satellite image or webcam before you go.
      • Data is refreshed hourly in the cloud; the weather models typically publish new runs every 1–6 hours.
